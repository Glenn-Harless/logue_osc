# FM Synthesis Notes for Kalimba Timbres

## Why FM Works for Tine Instruments

Kalimba tines produce a strong fundamental plus clusters of inharmonic partials. Two-operator FM emulates this efficiently:
1. The carrier supplies the fundamental while the modulator creates the higher, slightly detuned overtones.
2. Small ratio adjustments move sidebands in and out of alignment, mimicking how tine length and pickup placement change tone.
3. Independent amp and modulation decays model how real tines brighten on impact and quickly mellow as energy transfers to the resonator box.

## Curated Tine Ratios

The oscillator sweeps through a table of ratios chosen from spectral analyses of acoustic kalimbas:

- 1.50 – Perfect fifth tint; round and mellow
- 1.70 – Slightly stretched, adds gentle shimmer
- 1.90 – Strong tine bite without harshness
- 2.15 – Upper third cluster common in large kalimbas
- 2.40 – Balanced brightness for ensemble patches
- 2.70 – Airy, modern pluck
- 3.10 – Focused, mid-forward tone
- 3.50 – Bright stage kalimba character
- 4.00 – Glassy upper partials
- 4.60 – Cutting sparkle, good for leads
- 5.20 – Metallic chime territory

Interpolating across these anchors keeps sweeps musical while still covering the inharmonic territory that gives thumb pianos their charm.

## Modulation Depth & Tone Evolution

Modulation index (`I`) tracks perceived brightness. Values around 0.5–2 generate ample tine harmonics without overwhelming the fundamental. Higher settings (>6) move toward synthetic chimes and should be paired with short Tone Decay to avoid aliasing.

The Tone Decay parameter alters the mod envelope constant so brightness falls off sooner than loudness—mirroring how a real tine hands energy to the resonator box. Equal amp and tone decay keeps the metallic component persistent for more modern, sustained plucks.

## Body Resonance Approximation

Instead of another oscillator, a one-pole follower captures the average motion of the excitation. Mixing this back in simulates the soft, hollow body resonance. Lower damping (Body ≥ 60) gives the warm, boxy character heard in mic’d acoustic kalimbas.

## Attack Noise Considerations

Plucked thumb pianos exhibit finger noise and tine scrape. A short noise burst (3–45 ms) layered on the front recreates this feel. Keep Noise under 30 for natural articulation; higher values work for sampled/lo-fi aesthetics.
